Mapping UX at Enterprise Scale

In enterprise systems, UX drives adoption beyond customers. A new study, alongside four years of surveys, revealed long-standing issues that were impacting user satisfaction and system efficiency.

Role

Lead UX Researcher & Designer

Timeline

Team

The Challenge

Enterprise software faces unique UX challenges. Unlike consumer products, adoption isn't always voluntary, and users range from novices to power users. After analyzing four years of user satisfaction surveys, patterns emerged showing consistent pain points in navigation, information architecture, and workflow efficiency.

The central question became: How might we improve the user experience at scale while accommodating diverse user needs and existing workflows?

Research & Discovery

The research phase combined quantitative and qualitative methods to build a comprehensive understanding of user needs and pain points.

Research Activities

  • Analyzed 4 years of user satisfaction survey data (2,000+ responses)
  • Conducted 25 in-depth user interviews across different roles
  • Performed task analysis and workflow mapping sessions
  • Created personas representing key user segments
  • Competitive analysis of enterprise UX best practices

Key Insights

Navigation Complexity

Users reported spending 30% of their time navigating through menus rather than completing tasks.

Information Overload

Critical information was buried under layers of less relevant data, causing decision fatigue.

Workflow Friction

Common workflows required switching between 5-7 different screens, breaking concentration.

Learning Curve

New users needed 2-3 weeks to become proficient due to inconsistent patterns.

Design Solution

Based on research insights, I developed a comprehensive redesign focused on three core principles:

1. Task-Oriented Navigation

Restructured the information architecture around user tasks rather than system logic, reducing navigation depth by 40%.

2. Progressive Disclosure

Implemented a layered approach to information display, showing essential data upfront with easy access to details when needed.

3. Contextual Workflows

Created unified workflow views that keep users in context, reducing screen transitions by 60%.

Learnings & Reflections

This project reinforced the importance of long-term data collection in enterprise environments. The four years of survey data provided invaluable context that wouldn't have been visible in a shorter research timeline.

Working with diverse user groups taught me to design for flexibility while maintaining consistency. The solution needed to serve both novice users who needed guidance and power users who valued efficiency.

Perhaps most importantly, this work demonstrated that enterprise UX isn't about making things "pretty" — it's about making complex systems usable, reducing cognitive load, and ultimately helping people do their jobs better.
