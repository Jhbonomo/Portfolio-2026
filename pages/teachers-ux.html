What school teachers taught me about people and UX

Project

UX researcher for an EdTech platform

TIMELINE

2022

TEAM

UX research team

COMPANY

Newsela (Contractor via BairesDev)

My role

As a UX researcher contractor at BairesDev, I worked with Newsela’s UX research team conducting user interviews with teachers. I took part in generative and evaluative research, moderating interviews, synthesizing findings, and collaborating with the team to translate insights into product discussions and decisions.

These conversations took place in 2022, as students were returning to in-person learning after extended periods of remote education. What emerged was not just a snapshot of product usage, but a broader picture of adaptation for teachers, students, and the tools supporting them.

Research Setup

Methods

  • Generative user interviews (45-60minutes)
  • Evaluative interviews for product validation
  • Qualitative thematic analysis

Participants

K-12 teachers using leveled-reading tools, with varied experience levels and classroom contexts during the post-pandemic transition.

Research Focus

  • Differentiating instruction for different types of learners
  • Classroom challenges caused by increased uneven literacy levels after the pandemic
  • How digital tools supported (or failed) to support real classrooms
  • Alignment between Newsela's content, learning mediums, curriculum requirements, and teaching goals

The Challenge

Teachers described a classroom transformed: students returning with uneven knowledge, fragmented focus, and varying levels of digital fluency and sociability.

The challenge wasn't just about improving a product, it was about understanding how to support teachers and students navigating a post-pandemic educational landscape.

Teaching became less about delivering information and more about meeting learners where they were, making space for exploration, and supporting different paths toward understanding.

Key insights

Meet students where they are

Through thematic analysis, a clear pattern emerged: teaching had shifted from delivering information to meeting learners where they were, supporting exploration, recovery, and uneven paths toward understanding.

Teachers were no longer teaching a single classroom rhythm, but managing multiple learning stages simultaneously.

Spark curiosity through scaffolding

Engagement proved to be a critical driver of learning. Interfaces that invited exploration helped transform browsing into understanding. This insight informed a stronger focus on scaffolded learning, where:

  • Guided flows break down complexity
  • Timely hints act as just-in-time lessons
  • Progress tracking builds confidence over time

Research Impact on the Product

Insights from teacher interviews directly informed product directions and design discussions:

  • Curriculum alignment: Teachers consistently evaluated content based on how clearly it matched local curricula. Strong alignment reduced the cognitive effort required to assess relevance and suitability for classroom use.
  • Effectiveness of learning mediums: Research explored how different formats, audio, text, visual and interactive materials supported comprehension and engagement. Teachers emphasized that effectiveness varied widely depending on student learning styles.
  • Scaffolding learning tools: Educators highlighted the value of tools such as annotations, flashcards, and structured reading techniques. These insights contributed to approaches focused on more interactive and engaging experiences, including mixing visualizations within timelines and using progressively complex imagery.

Together, these findings reinforced the need to design for diverse types of learners rather than a single "average" user, shaping how engagement, clarity, and instructional support were framed in product conversations.

Reflections: The parallels between learning and UX

The way teachers navigated these challenges echoed how users engage with digital systems: entering with different assumptions, skills, and needs, not as blank slates, but as learners in motion.
